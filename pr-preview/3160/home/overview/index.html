    What is Tractus-X EDC?¶

    Tractus-X EDC is a dataspace connector: a piece of software every company runs to take part in a dataspace such as Catena-X, Factory-X, Construct-X or any other dataspace that speaks the Dataspace Protocol (DSP) and uses the Decentralized Claims Protocol (DCP) for authorization. It lets a company offer data to partners under conditions it defines, and obtain data from partners under conditions they define, without uploading anything to a central platform.

    It is the Eclipse Tractus-X distribution of the Eclipse Dataspace Components (EDC) connector. EDC is a framework; Tractus-X EDC turns it into ready-to-run Docker images and Helm charts, and adds what dataspaces need: verifiable credential checks, EDR caching with token refresh, connector discovery and more. Dataspace-specific rules, such as the Catena-X policies and the Business Partner Number (BPN) identity, are added as extensions. See Supported dataspaces.

    The problem it solves¶

    Two companies want to share data, for example a supplier's quality data with a car maker. Both need answers to the same questions before a single byte moves:

    • Who are you? The provider must be sure who is asking, without a shared user database.
    • Are you allowed? Only partners who hold the right credentials (in Catena-X, for example, membership and a signed framework agreement) or who belong to a given business partner group may see an offer.
    • Under which terms? Usage purpose, duration and other conditions must be agreed and recorded.
    • How do I get the data? Once agreed, the consumer needs a secure, short-lived way to call the provider's backend.

    A connector answers all four, in a standard way, so every participant can talk to every other participant.

    The four ideas you need¶

    • Catalog and offers

      A provider registers assets (pointers to its backend APIs), attaches policies, and publishes them as contract offers in its catalog. Consumers browse the catalog.

    • Contract negotiation

      The consumer requests an offer. The provider checks the consumer's credentials against the policy and, if all is fine, both sides store the same contract agreement.

    • Transfer and EDR

      With an agreement the consumer starts a transfer process. For pull transfers it receives an Endpoint Data Reference (EDR): an endpoint plus a short-lived token for the provider's data plane.

    • Decentralised identity

      Nobody logs in. Each connector proves who it is with verifiable credentials held in its own wallet, using the Decentralized Claims Protocol (DCP) and Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs).

    Two planes¶

    A Tractus-X EDC deployment has two runtimes:

    Control plane Data plane
    Job Decides whether data may flow Moves the data
    Talks to Other control planes (Dataspace Protocol), your apps (Management API), wallets Your backend systems, consumers holding an EDR
    Stores Assets, policies, contract definitions, negotiations, agreements, transfer processes, EDRs Data flows and token state
    Scales with Number of negotiations and transfers Data volume

    Keeping them separate means the data path can scale and be placed close to the data, while the negotiation logic stays in one well-protected place. See Architecture for the full picture.

    What Tractus-X adds on top of EDC¶

    • Catena-X policies (for the Catena-X dataspace): the constraints of standard CX-0152 (Membership, FrameworkAgreement, UsagePurpose, BusinessPartnerNumber, BusinessPartnerGroup, …) with validation on creation. Read more
    • BPN identity (Catena-X): business partner numbers resolved to DIDs through the BPN-DID Resolution Service (BDRS), and business partner groups for access control.
    • EDR management: one call to negotiate and start a transfer, an EDR cache, and token refresh so consumers keep access without renegotiating. Read more
    • Connector discovery: find a partner's connector and supported protocol versions from its DID document.
    • Operations: PostgreSQL schema migrations, HashiCorp Vault integration, Helm charts, and backward compatibility for older Dataspace Protocol versions.

    The extension catalogue lists every Tractus-X module.

    What Tractus-X EDC is not¶

    • Not a wallet. Credentials are held by a credential service (wallet) that you operate or rent. The connector asks it for verifiable presentations.
    • Not a business application. The connector exposes APIs; a business application (or a tool such as the Tractus-X SDK) drives them.
    • Not a data store. Data stays in your backend; the data plane proxies or pushes it on request.
